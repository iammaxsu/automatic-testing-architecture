# FWK038 — In-Test Presence and Liveness Indication

## Statement

While a test is running, the framework shall communicate **two distinct
facts**, to two distinct audiences:

1. **Presence** — this DUT is under test. Do not operate it, do not power it
   off. Addressed to **anyone who encounters the machine**, including someone
   who did not start the test.
2. **Liveness** — the test is still progressing. It has not hung, crashed, or
   entered a dead loop. Addressed to **the operator watching the run**.

These are separate obligations. Satisfying one does not satisfy the other, and
a mechanism serving one shall not defeat the other.

### Presence

Presence shall be advertised on every channel available to a user who arrives
at the machine without a running console:

| Channel | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Status file | written for the duration of the run |
| MOTD hook | installed, so a new login sees the warning |
| Terminal broadcast | sent to logged-in terminals **other than the one running the test** |
| Desktop notification | attempted when an X display is present |

The broadcast shall **exclude the invoking terminal**. That terminal already
carries the liveness display, and a multi-line broadcast written over it
destroys the very indicator this requirement mandates.

### Liveness

Any step that can run for more than **10 seconds without other console output**
shall display an indicator that visibly changes at least once per second for as
long as that step is running.

The indicator shall:

- **not stop before the step it covers stops.** A nominal duration is a
  prediction, not a bound. When the step overruns, the indicator shall keep
  moving and shall say that it has overrun.
- **not assert a state it cannot know.** It shall not report completion,
  idleness, or a final elapsed figure while the step is still running.
- **erase to end of line on each redraw**, so a shorter frame cannot leave the
  tail of a longer one on screen.
- **be bounded by a backstop**, so an indicator orphaned by a killed worker
  terminates rather than spinning forever.

A silent console for longer than the threshold, during a run that is in fact
healthy, is a defect under this requirement — the operator cannot distinguish
it from a hang, and the reasonable response to a suspected hang is to kill the
run, destroying hours of results.

## Rationale

The two facts are easy to conflate because one banner can carry both words, but
they fail independently and they are consumed differently.

Presence is **static** information for someone who may arrive at any moment and
has no context. A single message, persistent and re-displayable, serves it: the
status file and MOTD exist precisely so a user logging in an hour into a run
still learns not to power the machine off.

Liveness is **dynamic** information for someone already watching. It is carried
by *change over time*, not by content. A message saying "test in progress" that
was printed forty minutes ago tells an operator nothing about whether the
process is still alive; only a display that moves does that.

Conflating them produced both bugs this requirement is written against:

- **BUG0049** — the presence broadcast was sent with `wall(1)`, which writes to
  every terminal including the operator's own. Ten lines of presence banner
  scrolled away the liveness bar, and the run looked hung. Presence defeated
  liveness.
- **BUG0050** — the liveness bar stopped at the step's *nominal* duration while
  the step continued, leaving the console silent for the overrun. Liveness was
  treated as a countdown of expected time rather than as evidence of continued
  execution.

The cost of a false hang is asymmetric and high. An operator who believes a
1000-cycle endurance run has hung will kill it; the results are lost and the
bench time is spent again. There is no comparable cost to an indicator that
keeps moving.

## Scope

| Applies to | Notes |
|------------|-------|
| `net_test.sh` | per-transfer progress bar; `_iperf3_progress` / `_progress_stop` |
| `disk_test.sh`, `dev_detect.sh`, `slp_test.sh` | `test_progress_set` per loop |
| `function.sh` | `_adlink_broadcast`, status file, MOTD hook, X notification |
| `power_cycle.py`, `reboot.py`, `sleep_test.py` | control-node side; the DUT-side notification is FWK032 |

Out of scope: what the notification *says* on each OS, which is FWK032; and
non-interactive runs with no terminal at all (cron, systemd, Ansible), where
presence still applies and liveness has no audience.

## Implications

1. A per-loop notification is **not** a liveness indicator. `test_progress_set`
   fires once per iteration; for a single-iteration run it fires once, at the
   start. It satisfies presence, not liveness.
2. A liveness indicator must be stopped explicitly by its caller, not waited
   on — waiting on an indicator that correctly outlives its nominal duration
   would block until the backstop.
3. `wall(1)` is unusable for presence as-is, since it cannot exclude a terminal.
   The framework writes to `who(1)`'s terminals directly instead.
4. The invoking terminal must be captured when the library is sourced. By the
   time a broadcast runs it may be inside a background subshell where `tty(1)`
   reports no terminal, and the exclusion would silently stop working.
5. Where no terminal can be identified at all, nothing is excluded — there is
   no operator console to protect.

## Verification

`src/bash-shell/test_broadcast_tty.sh`:

- **Presence** — the invoking terminal receives nothing while another terminal
  receives the warning; with no TTY of our own, nothing is excluded; no bare
  `wall(1)` call remains; the invoking TTY is captured at source time.
- **Liveness** — a step whose real duration exceeds its nominal one keeps
  producing frames past the nominal mark, and those frames are marked as an
  overrun rather than reporting completion; each frame erases to end of line;
  `_progress_stop` terminates the indicator and leaves the line clean.

Gaps knowingly outstanding at `status: implementing`: short waits between
phases (`sleep 4` after a link-speed change, `_iperf_wait_ready`) are silent but
below the 10 s threshold. `test_progress_set`'s per-loop cadence is unchanged
and remains presence-only.
